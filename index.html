Expertise

The class I and class II antigen processing and presentation pathways are the arbiters of what T-cells can or can't target. This sensitive biological system needs to be understood in a tissue-specific context and is of critical importance to immunotherapy efficacy and resistance. My support turns antigen presentation biology from an obstacle into a resource that moves your project forward!

Understanding cancer requires understanding tissue-specific biology, fundamentals of cell proliferation and differentiation, as well as evolution through natural selection. Positive clinical outcomes build on complex concepts such as the cancer immunity cycle, immunoediting, and defining promising disease-specific targets based on the available data. With my support complex cancer biology becomes accessible, sharpening your toolset to fight the disease!

Genomics, transcriptomics, immunopeptidomics, proteomics, shmoteomics — contemporary tools are ample and complex. The difficult part is to know where to apply them and make them fall in line. With my support we will organize workflows and extract complementary evidence in your multi-omics setting!

Immunopeptidomics is the king of exploring and validating the landscape of presented by the antigen processing and presentation pathways. Clinical context make everything harder: personalization, short turnaround times, scarce samples, an obligation towards patients and their families to maximize data extraction, and an exceptionally high confidence thresholds. With my support we can enhance the compatibility of your plans with clinical pipelines and maximize translational value!

Solutions for you

Solutions for you — AI draft, pending review

I know your pain. Let's figure it out together.

Every translational programme runs into forks where the wrong call is only visible a year and a budget later. Eight of them are on the right.

Pick the one that sounds like your project.

You want to…

…triage candidates for immunogenicity

The decision

Your pipeline returns fifty predicted neoantigens. Each one costs peptide synthesis, multimer reagents and weeks of T-cell work. Advancing all fifty is the expensive way to discover that forty were never on the cell surface.

My part

I rank candidates on evidence of presentation, not on binding score alone — measured immunopeptidomes, expression, processing plausibility, and published reference data.

What you get

Your synthesis and assay budget goes to the ten candidates a T-cell can actually see, and you learn which ten before the money is spent!

…confirm your targets are actually presented

The decision

A prediction says a peptide binds. It does not say the peptide was ever produced, transported, loaded or displayed. That gap is usually found at validation — the most expensive place to find it.

My part

I close it with measurement. Immunopeptidomics turns a candidate list into an observation, and I know when the instrument can settle the question, when a reference dataset already has, and when the honest answer is that your material cannot support the experiment.

What you get

Targets that carry observed evidence instead of computed hope — a difference your next reviewer, partner or investor sees immediately!

…know whether your tumour can still present at all

The decision

A tumour that has lost part of its antigen presentation machinery cannot display your target however good the target is. Learn that after enrolment and you have spent a trial arm on a question you could have asked at screening.

My part

I assess class I and class II pathway integrity in your models and patient material — HLA loss of heterozygosity, transporter and proteasome defects, interferon signalling — and tell you what the tissue can still present.

What you get

You select the models and patients where your therapy has a mechanism to work, and you can explain your non-responders instead of guessing at them!

…audit your target discovery workflow

The decision

You have a discovery pipeline that works and no outside view of where it leaks. The steps that lose you candidates are rarely the steps you suspect, and every month it runs unexamined is a month of output you cannot fully defend.

My part

I walk the whole path — sample handling, acquisition, search settings, filtering thresholds, prioritisation logic — and tell you where the losses are, which thresholds are costing you real targets, and which steps are already fine.

What you get

A prioritised list of what to fix first, and an honest statement of what your pipeline can and cannot currently claim!

…clarify conflicting messages from different data sources

The decision

The exome calls the mutation. The transcript is barely expressed. The mass spectrometer never saw it. Three layers, three answers, and a target list due this week.

My part

I have spent my career reconciling genomics, transcriptomics and immunopeptidomics against each other. I can tell you which disagreement is biology, which is a limit of detection, and which is a processing artefact.

What you get

One defensible target list instead of three competing ones — and you know exactly how much confidence each entry has earned!

…scale an approach from one patient to a population

The decision

A workflow that works for one patient is not a product. Personalised discovery is per-sample cost and per-sample turnaround; a population approach needs shared targets — and nobody wants to fund the wrong one of the two.

My part

I separate the targets that recur across patients from the ones that are truly private, using immunopeptidomics reference data alongside your own, and I tell you what a shared-target approach would actually cover in your indication.

What you get

A clear split between what must stay personalised and what can be made off-the-shelf, with a defensible estimate of the population your shared targets would reach!

…decide whether to build immunopeptidomics in-house

The decision

Clinical immunopeptidomics needs instrument time, sample amounts you may not have, and a turnaround short enough to matter to a patient. Building it in-house is a two-year commitment, usually made in a single meeting.

My part

I map your real requirements — sample mass, depth, turnaround, regulatory context — against what a workflow can deliver, and against what an established laboratory could deliver for you tomorrow.

What you get

You build only what is worth building, and the capability you end up with matches the clinical question you actually have!

…make your science land with a non-specialist room

The decision

Your data are sound and the room does not follow. A steering committee, a regulator, a reviewer or a non-specialist investor decides on what they understood, not on what you know.

My part

I translate antigen presentation and immunopeptidomics for the audience in front of you — talks, figures, grant and manuscript text, or a rehearsal where I ask the questions they will.

What you get

Your work is judged on its merit, because the people deciding can finally see what you see!

About me

Born and raised in Switzerland, I received my BSc and MSc education at the University of Zurich where I was first exposed to mass spectrometry methods. There, I worked on understanding the pharmacokinetics of new psychoactive substances in the Department of Forensic Toxicology with help of MALDI-MS and LC-MS. The world of mass spectrometry was captivating enough to move into industry and join Biognosys in building their immunopeptidomics capabilities. The end-to-end workflows I built were a great introduction into the field and relied heavily on building pragmatic technical know-how for mass spectrometry hard- and software. Immunopeptidomics turned from a task to a burning interest. To refine my expertise and expand it to clinical applications I joined Prof. Michal Bassani-Sternberg's lab at the University of Lausanne to obtain my PhD in Cancer and Immunology. My work centered around the challenges that antigen prioritization pipelines face and where immunopeptidomics can maximize its impact in these complex proteogenomics pipelines to select immunogenic, cancer-specific targets — and, if necessary, from NGS data only. This demanded a deep understanding cancer immunology, antigen processing biology, immunogenicity screening, and practical conditions and challenges that target discovery pipelines face. I then joined Parallel Squared Technology Institute in Boston to support their development of next-generation mass spectrometry technologies as an expert for cancer biology, immunotherapy, proteogenomics, and immunopeptidomics method development. Currently I am [current role — pending].

Privately I am an avid traveler and language learner that is fascinated by this beautiful planet (and soon solar system?) we call home. My passion for sailing provides ample opportunity to reflect on how far a well-devised course can bring you, and that stormy waters make the best sailors!
